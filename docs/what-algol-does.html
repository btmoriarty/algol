What Algol does, in plain terms

It runs the review, so you can trust it

Algol does not review your code and it does not replace your review tools. It runs the process around them, so a change gets the right amount of scrutiny and every decision is honest and remembered.

✎

A change comes in

Someone edits the code. A migration, a password check, a typo, anything.

⚖

Algol matches the effort to the risk

Hard to undo gets a hard look. A change that could break something expensive, like deleting data or touching payments, is sent for a deep review on its own. A typo is not. You stop guessing how carefully to check.

⚙

It runs the reviewers you already use

No new reviewer to learn. Algol points each change at the review tools your team already runs, and collects what they find.

▤

Every finding lands in one honest list

Checked is never confused with guessed. Instead of scattered results, you get one record, and each finding is labeled by how sure it is.

✓

You decide, and the reason sticks

Nothing is decided for you. A person makes the call. If you wave something through, Algol saves why, and the one thing that would make it worth another look.

How sure is each finding?
A guessA scanner spotted a pattern. Worth a look, not a verdict.
Backed upA review panel agreed. Stronger, but still judgment.
ProvenA test or check confirmed it. Only this is called proven.
The one rule underneath it all: a guess is never quietly upgraded to proven. Two tools agreeing is still not proof. That is the whole point, you can trust the label, because Algol will not overstate it.
